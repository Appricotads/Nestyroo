Once your baby starts to roll, crawl and pull themselves up, your home looks different. Cupboards become interesting, cords become targets and stairs become a challenge. Baby proofing doesn't have to mean buying one of everything. Some of the most important steps cost nothing.

This guide walks through your home room by room and shows what to fix first, what's free and what's worth spending money on. It's general information and doesn't replace supervision. *Last updated 25 November 2026.*

<h2 id="rule">The big rule: supervision comes first</h2>

Baby proofing reduces risk, but it doesn't replace watching your child. Water is the clearest example. Water safety depends on active adult supervision whenever children are around baths, pools, ponds, dams, rivers, creeks and even buckets with water.

<h2 id="free">Free fixes first</h2>

Before you buy anything, try these:

- **Get down to baby's level.** Crawl around your home and look for what baby can reach.
- **Move things up.** Store medicines, cleaning products and chemicals high up, out of reach and out of sight.
- **Keep small objects away.** Coins, batteries, magnets and small toys can be swallowed.
- **Move furniture** away from windows and away from cords.
- **Keep hot drinks away** from the edge of tables and benches.

<h2 id="kitchen">Kitchen</h2>

- **Keep children away from anything hot**, including ovens, heaters, hot liquids and hot food. Cook on the back burners and turn pot handles inward.
- **Store poisons and cleaning products** high up, with child-resistant locks if they're in a low cupboard.
- **Give baby their own cupboard** with safe items, such as plastic containers, so they have something to explore.
- **Lock the oven and low cupboards** that contain anything unsafe. Simple locks are inexpensive.

<h2 id="bathroom">Bathroom and laundry</h2>

- **Never leave a baby alone in the bath**, even for a moment.
- **Keep the bathroom and laundry doors closed**, and empty buckets and baths after use.
- **Check the water temperature** before putting baby in.
- **Store medicines and cleaning products** up high or locked away.

<h2 id="living">Living areas</h2>

- **Secure tall furniture and TVs.** Fixing them to the wall stops them tipping if a child climbs.
- **Cover sharp corners** if baby is at the pulling-up stage.
- **Keep cords out of reach**, including phone chargers.
- **Blind and curtain cords.** These can be dangerous for young children. Keep them out of reach, and keep cots, beds and furniture away from them. Loose cords can be made safe with a safety device. Australia has mandatory standards for corded internal window coverings, so check new blinds have warning labels and that cords are secured. See your state's consumer protection or fair trading website for guidance.
- **Button batteries.** Keep remotes, toys and devices with button batteries out of reach. If you think a child has swallowed one, treat it as an emergency and call 000.

<h2 id="bedrooms">Bedrooms</h2>

- **Check the cot** meets the Australian standard and has a firm, correctly sized mattress.
- **Keep the cot away from windows and cords.**
- **Follow safe sleep advice** from Red Nose, including a clear cot with no pillows, bumpers or loose bedding.
- **Anchor dressers and bookshelves** so they can't tip.

<h2 id="outdoors">Stairs and outdoors</h2>

- **Use a safety gate** at the top and bottom of stairs once baby is mobile. Check it's installed correctly and that the child can't climb or open it. Gates don't have an Australian Standard, so read reviews and test it in store.
- **Keep doors and gates to outdoor areas closed.**
- **Check the garden** for poisonous plants, sharp tools and open water.
- **Pools and spas** need compliant fencing and constant adult supervision. Check your state's pool safety rules.

<h2 id="spend">Where to spend, where to save</h2>

| Spend on | Often fine to save on |
|---|---|
| Safety gates for stairs | Cushioned corner guards (use a folded towel or tape temporarily) |
| Furniture and TV anchors | Plug covers, if you have few accessible sockets |
| Cupboard locks for dangerous items | Locks on every drawer |
| A smoke alarm that works | Fancy baby-proofing kits you don't need |

Look for second-hand gates and locks only if they're in good condition and you have the fittings and instructions. Compare prices before you buy, and watch sales. <h2 id="emergency">Emergency numbers</h2>

Save these in your phone:

- **Emergency (police, ambulance, fire):** 000
- **Poisons Information Centre:** 13 11 26
- **Health Direct Australia:** 1800 022 222

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### When should I start baby proofing?
Before baby is mobile. Many parents start when baby begins rolling or sitting, and finish before crawling.

### What's the most important thing to baby proof first?
Poisons and medicines, water, and anything that could tip over or strangle, such as blind cords. Start with the free fixes.

### Do I need to buy a baby proofing kit?
Not usually. A kit often includes items you won't use. Buy the specific items you need after you've looked around your home.

### Are baby gates required by law?
There's no Australian Standard for safety gates, so check the product, install it correctly and look for good reviews. Pool fencing is covered by state rules.

### Is it safe to leave baby alone in a safe area?
Babies need supervision. Baby proofing lowers the risk, but it can't make a space safe enough to leave a young child unattended.

### What should I do if my child swallows something dangerous?
Call 000 straight away for a button battery or magnet, or the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26 for other poisons, and follow their advice.
