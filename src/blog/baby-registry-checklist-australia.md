A baby registry is a list of what you'd like for your baby, so friends and family can buy things you'll actually use instead of guessing. Done well, it saves you money and avoids a pile of duplicates. Done badly, it becomes a copy of every "must-have" list on the internet.

This guide gives you a practical baby registry checklist for Australia: what to add, what to leave off, and how to include gifts at different budgets. *Last updated 23 November 2026.*

<h2 id="what-for">What a baby registry is for</h2>

A registry does three jobs:

- **It tells guests what you need**, so you don't end up with five newborn outfits in the same size.
- **It lets people choose their own budget.** A good list has small, medium and big items.
- **It gives you a plan.** Writing the list forces you to decide what you really need and what can wait.

Remember that you don't have to register for everything. Plenty of parents leave big items off the list and buy them themselves, especially if they've spent time researching a pram or car seat.

<h2 id="where">Where to make a baby registry in Australia</h2>

Several Australian retailers offer gift registries, including [Baby Bunting](https://www.babybunting.com.au/gift-registry), Myer and Baby Village. Each works a little differently, so check how guests buy, what delivery costs, and what the retailer's returns terms are before you commit.

Two things are worth checking before you pick one:

- **Does it include the items you want?** A registry limited to one retailer's range may not have your chosen pram or cot.
- **Can you add items from other stores?** Some registries only let you add products from the retailer's own range, while others let you add links to any website.

If a registry doesn't suit, a shared wishlist or a simple list you send to family also works.

<h2 id="checklist">The baby registry checklist</h2>

Use this as a starting point and cross out whatever doesn't apply. Our [newborn and baby essentials checklist](/blog/newborn-essentials-checklist-australia/) explains what you need in the first weeks and what can wait.

| Category | Good to add | Think twice |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep** | Cot or bassinet, fitted sheets (a few), swaddles or sleeping bags | Pillows, quilts, cot bumpers and loose bedding (not recommended for safe sleep) |
| **Feeding** | Bottles and a steriliser if you plan to use them, burp cloths, bibs, a nursing pillow or breastfeeding supplies | Large bottle sets before you know what bub will take |
| **Changing** | Change mat, nappy bin, wipes, nappy cream, a first pack of nappies | Huge stockpiles of one size |
| **Bathing** | Baby bath or bath support, hooded towels, gentle wash, soft washcloths | Lots of products before you know what suits bub's skin |
| **Clothing** | A few bodysuits and sleepsuits in a range of sizes, socks, a hat | Lots of newborn size, because babies outgrow it fast |
| **Getting around** | Pram, car seat, a baby carrier, a nappy bag | Accessories you can buy later |
| **Home and safety** | Baby monitor, night light, a thermometer | Gadgets that duplicate what you already have |
| **Play (later)** | A play mat, soft books, a few simple toys | Piles of toys, which will come as gifts anyway |
| **Hospital and recovery** | Items from our [hospital bag checklist](/blog/hospital-bag-checklist-australia/), maternity pads, comfy clothes | Anything you'd rather choose yourself |

<h2 id="big-ticket">Big-ticket items: register, buy yourself, or share?</h2>

Prams, car seats and cots are the most expensive things on any list. You have three options:

1. **Add them to the registry** so guests can chip in. Some registries let several people contribute to one item.
2. **Buy them yourself** after you've researched them, and use sales events to save. See our guides to [prams](/blog/pram-buying-guide-australia/), [car seats](/blog/car-seat-buying-guide-australia/) and [cots](/blog/cot-buying-guide-australia/), and our post on whether to [buy now or wait for Black Friday](/blog/pram-car-seat-black-friday-wait-or-buy-australia/).
3. **Ask grandparents or close family** if they'd like to give one bigger gift together. Make it easy by naming the exact model you've chosen.

Car seats need extra care. The right seat depends on your child's age and size and on your car, and it should be fitted properly. Registering a specific model only works if you've checked it fits your car first.

<h2 id="skip">What to skip or delay</h2>

- **Lots of newborn-size clothing.** Babies grow quickly, so ask for a mix of sizes.
- **Single-use gadgets.** Wipe warmers and similar extras rarely earn their space.
- **Anything that goes against safe sleep guidance.** Pillows, bumpers and loose bedding aren't recommended. Red Nose has current safe sleep advice.
- **Toys for a newborn.** You will get plenty of these as gifts anyway.
- **Items you can't return easily.** Check the retailer's returns policy before you add big items.

For ways to spend less on all of this, see our guide to [saving money on baby gear](/blog/save-money-on-baby-gear-australia/) and the [first-year baby budget](/blog/first-year-baby-budget-australia/).

<h2 id="safety">Safety checks for registry items</h2>

Some baby products have mandatory safety standards in Australia, including child car restraints, household cots, portable cots, and prams and strollers. Before you add an item:

- **Check the safety standard** for the product on [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/), and check for recalls.
- **Ask guests to buy new** for cots and car seats. A second-hand one's history is hard to verify.
- **Choose a cot that meets the standard** and fits a firm, correctly sized mattress.

<h2 id="tips">Tips for a registry that works</h2>

- **Add items at different price points.** Include a few small things, some mid-range, and a few big items.
- **Add more than you expect to receive.** Not every item will be bought, and you can remove things later.
- **Include practical gifts.** Meals, vouchers and help around the house are valuable in the first weeks.
- **Update it.** Remove what you've bought or been given, and add things you've realised you need.
- **Keep a thank-you list.** Note who gave what as gifts arrive, because it's hard to remember when you're sleep-deprived.
- **Share it at the right time.** Send the link with the invitation to your baby shower, or when people ask what you need.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### When should I create a baby registry?
There's no rule. Many parents set one up in the middle of pregnancy, once they've shared the news and have time to research. It's fine to start later, or earlier if you like to plan.

### How many items should I put on a baby registry?
A longer list than you expect to receive gives guests choice. Start with the items you most want and add more over time.

### Should I put a pram or car seat on my registry?
Only if you've chosen a specific model and checked it suits you. Car seats especially need to fit your child and your car. You can also buy these yourself and register for smaller things.

### Is it rude to ask for money or vouchers instead?
Not at all. Many families prefer vouchers or contributions toward a bigger item. Say so politely on the list or when people ask.

### Do I need a registry if I'm getting hand-me-downs?
You can keep it short. Register only for what you still need, and mention that you'd love pre-loved items too.

### Can I include second-hand items?
For things like clothing and toys, yes. Be careful with cots and car seats, where age, condition and recalls matter.
