*Last updated 4 November 2026.*

This pram buying guide helps Australian parents choose the right pram or stroller for their baby, their car and their budget. A pram is usually one of the biggest purchases for a new baby, so it pays to know what to look for before you shop, and when to buy it for less.

<h2 id="types">Types of prams and strollers</h2>

| Type | Best for | Things to consider |
|---|---|---|
| Full-size pram or stroller | Everyday use from birth | Usually the heaviest and biggest to fit in a boot |
| Compact or travel stroller | Travel, public transport and small cars | May not suit a newborn, so check the age rating |
| Jogging stroller | Parents who run or walk on rough ground | Large wheels take more space, and many are not suitable for young babies |
| Double or tandem stroller | Two children close in age | Check the width for doorways and the weight |
| Travel system | Newborns, with a matching car seat or capsule | Check that the capsule meets Australian standards |

Many families end up with two: a full-size pram for everyday use and a lightweight stroller for trips.

<h2 id="newborn">Can a newborn use a stroller?</h2>

Only if it's designed for it. A newborn needs to lie flat or in a bassinet or carrycot attachment. Check the manufacturer's age and weight guidance for each pram before you buy, and follow it.

<h2 id="what-to-check">What to check before you buy</h2>

1. **Safety standard.** Prams and strollers must meet the mandatory Australian safety standard. Look for compliance information on the product page.
2. **Brakes.** They should be easy to use, preferably with one foot.
3. **Harness.** A secure harness should fit your baby as they grow.
4. **Weight and folding.** Try folding it with one hand. Can you lift it into your car?
5. **Boot fit.** Measure your boot, or take the pram to your car to test it.
6. **Handlebar height.** It should suit the tallest person who will push it.
7. **Wheels and suspension.** Larger wheels handle footpaths and grass better. Small wheels suit smooth ground.
8. **Storage basket.** Check that it's big enough, and easy to reach.
9. **Sun cover.** A large canopy helps with Australian sun, and a UPF rating is a useful extra.
10. **Warranty and spare parts.** Find out who handles repairs in Australia.

<h2 id="test">Test it in a shop if you can</h2>

Pictures and reviews can't tell you how a pram feels. If you can, visit a baby store and try a few models:

- Push it with weight in it, in a straight line and around corners.
- Fold and unfold it a few times.
- Check that the seat reclines smoothly.
- Test the brake with one foot.
- Ask which model has stock and how long delivery takes.

Once you know which model you like, compare prices online.

<h2 id="safety">Safety tips</h2>

- Always use the brakes when you stop, and use the harness every time.
- Don't hang heavy bags on the handlebar, because the pram can tip.
- Don't use a pram on stairs or escalators with your baby in it.
- Check [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au) for recalls before you buy, especially if the pram is second-hand.
- If you're buying second-hand, check that it has all its parts, the brakes work, and the harness isn't frayed.

<h2 id="when-to-buy">When to buy a pram</h2>

Most parents buy a pram in the last few months of pregnancy. Big sales are a good time to buy, if the price is genuinely lower:

- **Black Friday:** Friday 27 November 2026
- **Cyber Monday:** Monday 30 November 2026
- **Boxing Day:** Saturday 26 December 2026
- **Click Frenzy and EOFY sales:** other sale periods through the year

<h2 id="price-check">How to check that a pram deal is real</h2>

1. Record the price at two or three retailers now.
2. Note the model name and number, because retailers sometimes sell a slightly different "sale version".
3. Check what's included, such as a bassinet, rain cover, cup holder or adapters. Extras can add to the price later.
4. Check delivery cost and return policy.
5. If the price isn't lower than the best recent price, wait.

<h2 id="checklist">Pram buying checklist</h2>

- [ ] Type chosen (full-size, compact, travel system or double)
- [ ] Suitable for your baby's age
- [ ] Safety standard and recalls checked
- [ ] Brakes, harness and fold tested
- [ ] Fits your boot
- [ ] Budget set and prices recorded
- [ ] Included extras and warranty checked
- [ ] Return policy and delivery time checked

<h2 id="faq">Pram buying FAQ</h2>

### What type of pram is best for a newborn?
One that lets your baby lie flat, such as a pram with a bassinet or carrycot, or a stroller that reclines fully. Check the manufacturer's guidance for your model.

### When should I buy a pram?
Usually in the last few months of pregnancy. If you can, buy during a major sale, but don't leave it too late.

### Do I need a travel system?
Not necessarily. It's convenient if you want a car seat that clicks onto the pram, but you can buy them separately. Check that they're compatible.

### Is it safe to buy a second-hand pram?
It can be, if it has no recalls, all parts work, and the brakes and harness are in good condition. Check Product Safety Australia first.

### How much should I spend on a pram?
It depends on your budget and how you'll use it. Set a limit first, then compare features. A higher price doesn't always mean a safer or better pram.

### How long does a pram last?
Often from birth until about 3 or 4 years old, depending on the type. A lightweight stroller may be useful later.
