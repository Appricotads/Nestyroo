*Last updated 5 November 2026.*

A cot is the one piece of nursery furniture your baby will use every day, so safety comes before style. This cot buying guide covers the types of cots, the safety checks that matter, how to choose a mattress and when to buy, so you can set up a safe sleep space for bub without overspending.

<h2 id="types">Types of cots and sleep spaces</h2>

| Type | Best for | Things to consider |
|---|---|---|
| Household cot | Everyday use from birth to toddler age | Takes up space, but lasts the longest |
| Portable (folding) cot | Travel, grandparents' homes and small spaces | Usually thinner mattress, so don't add extra padding |
| Convertible cot | Families who want one piece of furniture for years | Check each stage is safe and the conversion parts are included |
| Bassinet | The first few months | Outgrown quickly, so check the weight and age limit |

Many families use a bassinet first, then move to a cot. A cot can be used from birth, so you don't have to buy both.

<h2 id="standards">Safety standards</h2>

Household cots and portable cots sold here must meet a mandatory safety standard. Look for compliance information on the product page and the label. Don't buy a cot that doesn't state it meets the standard.

Bassinets and other baby sleepers don't have the same mandatory standard, so the safety checks below matter even more for those.

Check [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au) for recalls on any cot before you buy it, new or second-hand.

<h2 id="what-to-check">What to check before you buy</h2>

1. **Bar gaps.** The gaps between the bars should be between 50 mm and 95 mm. This helps stop a baby's head, arms or legs getting stuck.
2. **No gaps or footholds a child could climb.** Avoid decorations, cut-outs or ledges that give little feet something to grip.
3. **Sturdy construction.** Give the cot a shake. It shouldn't wobble, and the sides should lock firmly.
4. **Mattress fit.** The mattress should fit snugly, with no more than a small gap at the sides. Check the cot's instructions for the right mattress size.
5. **Height settings.** A base that lowers as your baby grows is useful. Lower it before your baby can sit or pull up.
6. **Finish.** Check that paint or finishes are non-toxic and that there are no sharp edges or splinters.
7. **Assembly.** Check the instructions, and that all the screws and parts are included. Re-tighten the screws now and then.
8. **Warranty and spare parts.** Find out who supports the product here.

<h2 id="mattress">Choosing a cot mattress</h2>

- **Firm and flat.** A firm mattress is recommended for safe sleep. Avoid soft or sagging mattresses.
- **Right size.** It should fit the cot snugly, so there are no big gaps at the edges.
- **Breathable and easy to clean.** Look for a cover you can wipe or wash.
- **Don't add extra padding.** A mattress topper, pillow, or soft bedding in the cot can be unsafe.
- **Buy new if you can.** Second-hand mattresses can have a hidden history, such as sagging or mould.

<h2 id="safe-sleep">Safe sleeping basics</h2>

Follow the latest safe sleeping guidance from [Red Nose](https://rednose.org.au). The main points are:

- Sleep baby on their back from birth.
- Keep baby's face and head uncovered.
- Keep the cot clear of pillows, soft toys, bumpers and loose bedding.
- Place baby with their feet at the end of the cot, and tuck any bedding in firmly, or use a safe sleeping bag.
- Keep the cot away from blind cords, curtains, heaters and windows.

Your midwife or child health nurse can answer questions about your baby's sleep.

<h2 id="second-hand">Second-hand cots</h2>

A second-hand cot can be fine, but check carefully:

- Is it a recalled model?
- Does it meet the current standard?
- Are all the parts there, and does it assemble firmly?
- Are there cracks, loose screws or damage?

If you're not sure, buy new. Don't pay for a cot with a missing or unknown history, and don't use a cot that has no instructions.

<h2 id="faq">Cot FAQ</h2>

### Do I need a bassinet as well as a cot?
No. A cot can be used from birth, so a bassinet is optional. Some parents like one because it's small and portable in the early weeks.

### Can I use a portable cot every day?
Some are designed for regular use, but many are made for travel. Check the manufacturer's instructions, and don't add extra mattresses or padding.

### How long does a cot last?
Often until your child is about 2 to 3 years old, when they start to climb or move to a bed. Move to a bed sooner if they can climb out.

### Is a second-hand cot safe?
It can be, if it meets the standard, isn't recalled, and is in good condition with all its parts. If you're unsure, buy new.

### Do I need cot bumpers or a pillow?
No. Safe sleeping guidance says to keep the cot clear of bumpers, pillows and soft toys.

### When should I set up the cot?
A few weeks before the due date, so you have time to assemble it and check it.
