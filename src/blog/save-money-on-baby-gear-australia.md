*Last updated 12 November 2026.*

Babies don't need as much as the shops suggest, but what they do need adds up fast. This guide shows you how to save money on baby gear in Australia without cutting corners on safety: what to buy new, what to borrow or buy second-hand, how to time your purchases, and how to avoid the common traps.

<h2 id="list">1. Start with a list, not a shop</h2>

Most overspending happens before bub arrives, when everything looks essential. Write down what you actually need for the first three months, and leave the rest until you know what you use.

Our [newborn essentials checklist](/blog/newborn-essentials-checklist-australia/) is a good starting point. Anything that's not on your list can wait.

<h2 id="what-to-buy-how">2. Decide what to buy new, second-hand or borrow</h2>

| Item | Best way to get it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Car seat or capsule | Buy new | Safety history matters, and seats expire |
| Cot | Buy new, or second-hand only if you can confirm the standard and recalls | Mandatory safety standard applies |
| Cot mattress | Buy new | Second-hand mattresses can hide wear |
| Pram or stroller | New or second-hand in good condition | Check brakes, harness and recalls |
| Clothes | Second-hand or hand-me-downs | Babies outgrow them in weeks |
| Toys and books | Second-hand, library or toy library | Used briefly |
| Baby carrier, bouncer, play mat | Second-hand or borrow | Low risk if undamaged |
| Nappies and wipes | New, bought on special | Compare price per nappy |

For safety-critical items, see our guides to buying a [car seat](/blog/car-seat-buying-guide-australia/), [cot](/blog/cot-buying-guide-australia/) and [pram](/blog/pram-buying-guide-australia/).

<h2 id="timing">3. Time your big purchases</h2>

Big-ticket items such as prams, car seats and cots are the ones worth waiting for a sale on, as long as the discount is real.

- **Black Friday:** Friday 27 November 2026
- **Cyber Monday:** Monday 30 November 2026
- **Boxing Day:** Saturday 26 December 2026
- **EOFY sales:** around June each year

You'll still need to buy safety items in time for the birth, so don't leave a car seat until the last week. If your due date is soon, buy what you need now and check the retailer's returns policy. <h2 id="price-check">4. Track prices before you buy</h2>

A "sale" price isn't always a saving.

1. **Note today's price** at two or three retailers, with the date.
2. **Set a target price** you'd be happy with.
3. **Check the model number**, because some sale versions are slightly different.
4. **Add delivery and fees** to the total.
5. **Ask about price matching.** Some retailers will match a lower price elsewhere, so ask before you pay. Terms vary, so check the conditions.

If the price doesn't reach your target, wait. Another sale will come.

<h2 id="habits">5. Habits that save the most money</h2>

### Borrow and hand down
Friends and family often have gear to pass on. Ask before you buy, and offer to pass it on when you're done.

### Buy second-hand clothes
Babies often wear an outfit only a handful of times. Op shops, local buy-and-sell groups and second-hand shops are good sources. Wash everything before use.

### Buy the next size, not three sizes ahead
Babies grow at different rates, and it's hard to guess. Stock up on the size you need now, plus a couple of items in the next size.

### Compare the price per nappy
A bigger box isn't cheaper if your baby outgrows the size, and a headline discount can hide a worse unit price. Divide the price by the number of nappies.

### Use libraries and toy libraries
Libraries lend books, and toy libraries lend toys, so you don't pay for things used for a few months.

### Ask for what you need
Family and friends often want to help. A short wish list at a baby shower or for gifts means you get what you need, not what you don't.

### Check what support you may be eligible for
There are government payments and subsidies for families, such as paid parental leave, family tax benefit and child care subsidy. Eligibility and amounts change, so check the current details on the [Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au) website.

<h2 id="traps">6. Traps to avoid</h2>

- **Impulse buys during sales.** Only buy what's on your list.
- **Buy now, pay later without a plan.** Interest-free periods can end, and late fees add up. Only use it if you're certain you can pay it off on time.
- **Bundles you don't need.** A package deal is only a bargain if you'd have bought every item anyway.
- **Cheap, unfamiliar online sellers.** If a price is far below everyone else's, check whether the product meets Australian safety standards.
- **"Newborn" sizes in bulk.** Babies grow fast, so many only wear the smallest size for a few weeks.
- **Gadgets you don't need,** like wipe warmers and bottle sterilisers if a pot will do.

<h2 id="safety">7. What never to skimp on</h2>

Saving money matters, but not at the cost of safety. Spend what you need on:

- **A car seat that meets the standard** and is fitted correctly.
- **A cot or sleep space that meets the safety standard,** with a firm, fitted mattress.
- **A pram with working brakes and a secure harness.**

Check [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au) for recalls on any item you're buying, especially second-hand.

<h2 id="faq">Saving money on baby gear FAQ</h2>

### How much do I need to spend on baby gear?
It depends on your situation, so start with a list of what you actually need, and set a budget for each item. Borrowing and second-hand clothes and toys can cut the cost a lot.

### Is it safe to buy second-hand baby gear?
Clothing, books, toys and carriers are usually fine. For car seats, cots and prams, only buy second-hand if you know the full history, it meets the standard, and it hasn't been recalled.

### When is the best time to buy baby gear on sale?
Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Boxing Day and EOFY are good times for big items, if the price is genuinely lower than your recorded price.

### What baby items can I skip?
Wipe warmers, a change table (a mat on a firm surface works), lots of newborn clothes and most baby gadgets.

### Is buy now, pay later a good idea for baby gear?
Only if you can pay it off on time, because late fees and missed payments can cost more than you save.
