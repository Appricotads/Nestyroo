Most baby lists tell you what to buy but not when. That's how parents end up buying everything in the third trimester, then discovering half of it wasn't needed until months later.

This baby shopping list for Australia is organised by stage, so you can spread the cost and buy things when you actually need them. If you want the full what-do-I-need list, see our [newborn and baby essentials checklist](/blog/newborn-essentials-checklist-australia/). *Last updated 25 November 2026.*

<h2 id="how">How to use this list</h2>

Buy in the order you need things, not the order you see them in shops. Big items need research and often go on sale, so start early. Small items can wait until you know what suits your baby.

Everything is general guidance. Your needs depend on your home, your car, your budget and your baby.

<h2 id="early">Early pregnancy: research, don't buy</h2>

The first trimester is for planning, not shopping.

- **Make a rough budget.** Our [first-year baby budget](/blog/first-year-baby-budget-australia/) helps.
- **Open a savings account** for baby costs, if you'd like to keep the money separate. See our guide to a [savings account for baby costs](/blog/savings-account-for-baby-costs-australia/).
- **Start a list** of what you've already got, what friends can lend and what you need to buy.

<h2 id="second">Second trimester: start researching the big items</h2>

This is a good time to compare and research.

- **Pram.** Read our [pram buying guide](/blog/pram-buying-guide-australia/) and try a few in store.
- **Car seat.** Check the type that suits your car and your baby. See our [car seat buying guide](/blog/car-seat-buying-guide-australia/).
- **Cot or bassinet.** See our [cot buying guide](/blog/cot-buying-guide-australia/).

You don't have to buy yet. Watch prices, note the models you like and wait for a sale. Our post on [buying now or waiting for Black Friday](/blog/pram-car-seat-black-friday-wait-or-buy-australia/) explains how.

<h2 id="third">Third trimester: buy the essentials</h2>

Now buy what you must have in place before the birth.

- **A car seat that's fitted and ready.** You'll need it for the trip home from hospital.
- **A safe place for baby to sleep.** A cot or bassinet that meets the standard, with a firm, correctly sized mattress and fitted sheets.
- **Nappies and basic changing supplies**, starting with a small amount.
- **A small set of clothing** in a few sizes. Newborn size is outgrown quickly.
- **Feeding supplies** that suit your plan, such as breastfeeding items or bottles and a steriliser.
- **A [hospital bag](/blog/hospital-bag-checklist-australia/)**, packed a few weeks before your due date.

If you're setting up a registry, our [baby registry checklist](/blog/baby-registry-checklist-australia/) helps you choose what to add.

<h2 id="before">Final weeks before the birth</h2>

- Wash and put away baby clothes and bedding.
- Set up the sleep space and the change area.
- Check the car seat fits and that you know how to use it.
- Stock the freezer and pantry, because the first weeks at home are busy.

<h2 id="first">First three months: buy what you find you need</h2>

Wait until you know what suits your baby, then add:

- **A baby carrier or wrap**, if you want your hands free.
- **More nappies or clothes** in the sizes your baby is actually wearing.
- **A bath or bath support**, once the cord stump has healed.
- **Extra muslins and burp cloths** if baby is a spewy one.
- **Things that solve a problem you actually have.** If bub is unsettled at night, you'll know what helps once you've tried a few things.

<h2 id="six">Around six months: solids and mobility</h2>

- **A high chair**, once baby is ready for solids. See our [high chair buying guide](/blog/high-chair-buying-guide-australia/).
- **Bibs, bowls and spoons** designed for babies.
- **A play mat** and a few simple toys.
- **Safety items** as baby starts to move, such as gates and cupboard locks. See our guide to [baby-proofing on a budget](/blog/baby-proofing-on-a-budget-australia/).

<h2 id="money">Tips to spread the cost</h2>

- **Record the price** of the big items at two or three retailers, then watch for real discounts.
- **Use sale events.** Black Friday, Boxing Day, EOFY and Click Frenzy are common times for baby sales. See our [sales calendar guides](/category/sales-calendar/).
- **Borrow or buy second-hand** for clothes and play items, but buy new for car seats and cots.
- **Ask for what you need** as gifts, and see [how to save money on baby gear](/blog/save-money-on-baby-gear-australia/).
- **Don't buy ahead of need.** Babies change fast, and an item you bought in advance may not suit.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### What should I buy first for a baby?
Start with the items that must be ready for the first day: a fitted car seat and a safe sleep space. Everything else can follow.

### When should I start buying baby things?
Many parents start researching in the second trimester and buy the essentials in the third trimester. There's no fixed rule, so start when you're comfortable.

### Do I need everything before the baby arrives?
No. You need the safety essentials and a small set of basics. Add the rest once baby is here and you know what you need.

### What can wait until after the birth?
Most clothing beyond a few sets, extra gear and anything that depends on your baby's habits. If you're unsure, wait.

### How much should I buy in newborn size?
Only a small amount. Many babies move into the next size within weeks.

### Is it worth buying baby gear on sale?
Yes, if you've researched the item and the discount is real. Compare prices over a few weeks before you commit.
