*Last updated 17 November 2026.*

A baby changes your budget in ways that are easy to underestimate. This first-year baby budget guide shows the costs Australian parents should plan for, helps you work out your own numbers with a worksheet, and explains where you can save money without cutting corners on safety.

We don't give a set total, because costs vary a lot between families. Use this to build your own.

**In this guide:** [One-off costs](#one-off) · [Ongoing costs](#ongoing) · [Income changes](#income) · [Worksheet](#worksheet) · [Saving](#saving) · [Checklist](#checklist) · [FAQ](#faq)

<h2 id="one-off">One-off costs before and after the birth</h2>

These are the bigger purchases, often made before the baby arrives.

- **Safe sleep space:** a cot or bassinet, a firm mattress and fitted sheets. See our [cot buying guide](/blog/cot-buying-guide-australia/).
- **Car seat or capsule:** you need one to bring your baby home. See our [car seat buying guide](/blog/car-seat-buying-guide-australia/).
- **Pram or carrier:** see our [pram buying guide](/blog/pram-buying-guide-australia/).
- **Clothes, wraps and bedding:** a small set in two sizes.
- **Feeding supplies:** these depend on how you plan to feed.
- **Hospital bag items:** see our [hospital bag checklist](/blog/hospital-bag-checklist-australia/).
- **Birth and hospital costs:** check what's covered by Medicare and your private health cover, if you have it, and ask your hospital about any out-of-pocket costs.

Our [newborn and baby essentials checklist](/blog/newborn-essentials-checklist-australia/) shows what you really need.

<h2 id="ongoing">Ongoing costs through the year</h2>

- **Nappies and wipes:** a regular cost that grows as your baby does. Compare the price per nappy.
- **Feeding:** supplies and food as your baby starts solids.
- **Clothing:** babies outgrow sizes quickly.
- **Health and care:** GP visits, pharmacy items and any cover you choose. Check what's covered.
- **Child care:** if you plan to return to work, this can be one of the biggest costs. Find out about the Child Care Subsidy through Services Australia.
- **Higher household bills:** more laundry, heating or cooling, and groceries.
- **Transport:** petrol, parking and car costs.
- **Life insurance and household cover:** review these with a licensed adviser if your family situation has changed.

<h2 id="income">Changes to your income</h2>

Your income may change when baby arrives. Check what you may be eligible for through [Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au), such as paid parental leave, family tax benefit and child care subsidy. Eligibility and amounts change, so use the current details from the website. Also check your employer's parental leave policy.

<h2 id="saving">Ways to keep the first year affordable</h2>

- **Buy safety items new,** and save on clothes, toys and carriers by buying second-hand or borrowing.
- **Time big purchases for the sales,** such as Black Friday on 27 November 2026, Boxing Day on 26 December, and EOFY in June. Always compare against your recorded prices.
- **Compare the price per nappy,** not just the pack price.
- **Ask for what you need** at baby showers and from family.
- **Review your budget every few months.** Costs change as your baby grows.

<h2 id="faq">First-year baby budget FAQ</h2>

### How much does a baby cost in the first year?
It varies a lot between families, depending on income, child care, where you live and what you buy new or second-hand. Build your own budget with the worksheet.

### What is the biggest cost in the first year?
For many families, it's the loss of income while on leave, or child care when they return to work. Check the details for your situation.

### What can I save on?
Clothes, toys, carriers and some furniture can often be borrowed or bought second-hand. Safety items such as car seats and cot mattresses are better bought new.

### Are there government payments for new parents?
There can be, such as paid parental leave, family tax benefit and child care subsidy. Eligibility changes, so check [Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au).

### When should I start budgeting for a baby?
As early as you can. Starting in pregnancy gives you time to save and to buy at sale prices.
