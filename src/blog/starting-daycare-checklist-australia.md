Getting ready for your child's first day of daycare can feel like a big task. Between spare clothes, drink bottles, hats and everyday essentials, it's easy to wonder what you actually need to pack.

The good news is that a little preparation makes the transition easier for everyone. Every early learning centre has its own requirements, so this starting daycare checklist helps you organise the essentials, label your child's belongings and feel more confident about day one. *Last updated 27 November 2026.*

<h2 id="pack">What to pack for daycare</h2>

Before packing your child's bag, check the centre's welcome pack or ask the educators what they provide. Some centres supply nappies, wipes, meals and bedding, while others ask families to bring their own.

Here's a general checklist to get you started:

- **Spare clothes:** Pack at least one or two complete changes, including tops, bottoms, underwear and socks. Add extra outfits if your child is toilet training.
- **Water bottle:** Choose an easy-to-use, reusable bottle with your child's name clearly labelled.
- **Sun hat:** Pack a suitable sun-protective hat, such as a broad-brimmed or legionnaire-style hat.
- **Comfortable clothing:** Choose practical clothes that allow your child to move, play and explore. Consider the weather and the activities planned for the day.
- **Spare shoes:** If requested, pack an extra pair, particularly during wet weather or water-play activities.
- **Nappies and wipes:** Bring these if your centre doesn't supply them.
- **Rest-time items:** Check whether you need to provide sheets, a blanket or a familiar comfort item.
- **Lunchbox and snacks:** Only pack food if meals aren't provided. Ask about the centre's food and allergy policies first.
- **Wet bag:** A reusable wet bag is handy for storing clothes that become dirty or wet during the day.

For younger children, you might also need to pack a dummy in a labelled container, a familiar comforter or any other approved personal care items.

<h2 id="label">What to label</h2>

Labelling your child's belongings makes it easier for educators to identify their items and helps reduce mix-ups with other children's things.

Try to label:

- Clothing, including jumpers, jackets and spare outfits
- Shoes and socks
- Drink bottles and lunchboxes
- Backpacks and storage bags
- Hats
- Bedding, sheets and blankets
- Dummies and their containers
- Personal care items, where permitted

Use durable labels that can withstand regular washing and everyday use. For clothing, iron-on or stick-on fabric labels can be useful, while waterproof labels work well on drink bottles and lunch containers.

A helpful tip is to check that labels stay on after washing. It's also worth checking your child's bag regularly to replace missing or damaged labels.

For privacy and safety, avoid displaying your child's full name on the outside of clothing or items they wear when travelling to and from daycare.

<h2 id="organise">Tips for organising your daycare bag</h2>

A well-organised bag makes busy mornings easier and helps educators find what they need throughout the day.

A few simple habits can help:

- Choose a bag with enough room for daily essentials without being too bulky.
- Pack spare clothes in a separate pouch so they're easy to find.
- Keep wet or dirty clothes in a reusable wet bag.
- Use clearly labelled containers for smaller belongings.
- Check the bag each evening and replace anything that has been used.
- Keep a spare set of seasonal clothing ready at home.

If your child is old enough, involve them in packing their bag. This can help them become familiar with their belongings and feel more prepared for the new routine.

<h2 id="ask">What to check with your daycare centre</h2>

Not every centre follows the same packing requirements, so it's worth confirming a few details before the first day.

Ask educators:

- Are meals, snacks and drinks provided?
- Do families need to supply nappies and wipes?
- Are sheets and bedding supplied?
- Does the centre provide sunscreen, or should families bring their own?
- Are there restrictions on toys, food or comfort items from home?
- What is the procedure for bringing and administering prescribed medication?

Make sure educators also know about your child's allergies, dietary requirements, medical needs, sleep routine and any other important care information.

The Raising Children Network's guide to [starting child care](https://raisingchildren.net.au/grown-ups/work-child-care/planning-starting-child-care/starting-child-care) has further advice on preparing your child and sharing important information with educators.

<h2 id="day-one">Make the first day easier</h2>

Preparing your child's daycare bag the night before can take some pressure off the morning rush. Double-check the centre's checklist, make sure everything is labelled and leave enough time for a relaxed drop-off.

It's also worth arranging an orientation visit if the centre offers one. Getting familiar with the educators, rooms and daily routine can help your child feel more comfortable with the change.

You don't need to have everything perfect on the first day. Once you understand your centre's routine, you'll find it easier to adjust what you pack and create a system that works for your family.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### What should I pack for my child's first day of daycare in Australia?
Start with a bag, spare clothes, a labelled water bottle, a sun hat and any required personal items. Depending on your centre, you may also need to pack nappies, wipes, bedding, food or a comfort item.

### How many spare outfits should I pack for daycare?
One or two complete changes are a useful starting point. Younger children and those who are toilet training may need additional outfits, including spare socks and underwear.

### Do I need to label everything for daycare?
It's a good idea to label clothing, shoes, drink bottles, lunchboxes, bags, hats and bedding. This helps educators identify your child's belongings and reduces the chance of items getting mixed up.

### Does daycare provide nappies, food and bedding in Australia?
This depends on the individual early learning centre. Some provide nappies, meals and bedding, while others ask families to supply these items. Check with your centre before your child's first day.

### Can my child bring a favourite toy or blanket to daycare?
Many centres allow a familiar comfort item, such as a small blanket or soft toy, to help children settle. Policies vary, so ask educators what is permitted before packing personal toys.

<h2 id="you">Prepare yourself emotionally, too</h2>

Starting daycare isn't just a big milestone for your child. It can be one of the hardest separations for both little ones and their parents, especially for first-time parents.

You might spend weeks preparing their bag, labelling every little item and checking the centre's routine, only to find yourself feeling emotional when it's finally time to say goodbye. That's completely normal.

Your child may cry, cling to you or need a little extra reassurance during the first few drop-offs. You might find yourself checking your phone, wondering how they're going, or feeling a little lost without them around.

Give yourself some time to adjust, too. Trust the educators, allow your child time to settle into their new routine, and remember that feeling emotional doesn't mean you're making the wrong decision.

And here's a little tip: have a coffee ready for after drop-off. Sometimes a quiet moment, a warm cup and a little time to collect yourself are exactly what you need.

It's a big step for both of you, so be kind to yourself. You're both learning something new, one day at a time.
