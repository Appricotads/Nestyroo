*Last updated 5 November 2026.*

Packing your hospital bag early takes the stress out of the final weeks of pregnancy. This hospital bag checklist covers what to pack for you, your partner and your baby, the paperwork to bring, and what you can leave at home, so you're ready when labour starts.

Your hospital or birth centre will have its own list and rules, so check it first. Your midwife can answer any questions about what to bring.

<h2 id="when">When to pack</h2>

Many parents pack their bag by about 36 weeks, in case bub arrives early. Keep it by the door or in the car, and keep the paperwork and your phone charger somewhere easy to grab.

<h2 id="paperwork">Paperwork and essentials</h2>

- Medicare card and private health insurance details, if you have them
- Hospital admission forms and any booking paperwork
- Your pregnancy health record or antenatal notes
- Birth preferences, if you've written them down
- Phone and a long charging cable
- Wallet, with a bit of cash or a card for the car park and vending machines

<h2 id="for-you">For you</h2>

Think comfort and practical, because you'll probably be there for a day or more.

**During labour**
- Comfortable clothes you don't mind getting messy
- Hair ties
- Lip balm
- A water bottle with a straw
- Snacks and a drink you like
- Something for relaxation, such as headphones and a playlist

**After the birth**
- Maternity pads, and a few pairs of comfortable underwear
- Loose pyjamas or a nightie, and a robe
- Slippers or non-slip socks
- Nursing bras or loose, front-opening tops if you plan to breastfeed
- Toiletries: toothbrush, toothpaste, shampoo, hairbrush and deodorant
- Going-home outfit that's loose and comfortable
- A pillow from home, with a coloured case so it doesn't get mixed up

<h2 id="for-baby">For baby</h2>

The hospital usually provides some basics, but check what yours supplies.

- Going-home outfit, in two sizes (newborn and one size up)
- A few bodysuits and sleepsuits
- Hat and socks or booties
- Muslin wraps or a blanket
- Newborn nappies and a small pack of wipes
- A car seat or capsule that is already fitted in the car

Your baby can't go home in a car without a properly fitted restraint, so practise installing it before the birth. Our [car seat buying guide](/blog/car-seat-buying-guide-australia/) explains how to choose one and check the fit.

<h2 id="partner">For your partner or support person</h2>

- A change of clothes and something comfortable to sleep in
- Toothbrush and toiletries
- Phone charger
- Snacks and a drink
- Cash or a card
- Camera or a phone with plenty of storage, if you want photos

<h2 id="feeding">Feeding supplies</h2>

Every family's plan is different. Ask your midwife or lactation consultant what suits you.

- If you plan to breastfeed: nursing pads, nipple cream and a feeding pillow if you like one
- If you plan to bottle feed: check with your hospital about what they supply and what to bring

<h2 id="leave-at-home">What to leave at home</h2>

- Valuables and large amounts of cash
- Too many baby clothes. Babies grow quickly, and you can bring more later.
- Strong perfumes or scented products, as some hospitals ask you to avoid them
- Anything you'd be upset to lose or get stained

<h2 id="saving">Saving money on your hospital bag</h2>

You don't need to buy everything new.

- Borrow or buy second-hand clothing, wraps and pillows.
- Check what your hospital provides before you buy nappies, pads or toiletries.
- Wait for the sales for items you do want to buy. Black Friday is on 27 November 2026, and Boxing Day is on 26 December 2026. Compare against your recorded prices first. <h2 id="checklist">Quick checklist</h2>

- [ ] Medicare card, insurance details and hospital forms
- [ ] Antenatal notes and birth preferences
- [ ] Phone, long charger and wallet
- [ ] Comfortable clothes, pads and toiletries for you
- [ ] Going-home outfit for you and bub, in two sizes
- [ ] Nappies, wipes, wraps and a hat for bub
- [ ] Snacks and a water bottle
- [ ] Car seat fitted in the car
- [ ] Bag by the door by about 36 weeks

<h2 id="faq">Hospital bag FAQ</h2>

### When should I pack my hospital bag?
Many parents pack by about 36 weeks. If your baby may arrive early, pack sooner.

### Do I need to bring nappies and wipes?
Check your hospital. Many provide some, but it's good to bring a small pack in case.

### What size clothes should I pack for the baby?
Pack two sizes, such as newborn and one size up, because babies vary a lot at birth. Sizes also vary by brand.

### Do I need to bring a car seat to the hospital?
Yes. You need an approved, correctly fitted restraint to take your baby home. Fit it before the birth.

### Can my partner stay overnight?
It depends on the hospital and the ward. Ask your hospital in advance.

### What should I not pack?
Valuables, too many clothes and anything the hospital asks you not to bring.
