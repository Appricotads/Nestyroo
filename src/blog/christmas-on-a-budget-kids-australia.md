*Last updated 17 November 2026.*

Christmas can get expensive quickly, especially with little kids. This guide gives you 10 practical ways to do Christmas on a budget for your kids in Australia, from setting a limit before you shop to timing the sales, so you can enjoy the day without a bill hanging over January.

Christmas Day is on Friday 25 December 2026, so there's still time to plan.

<h2 id="1-limit">1. Set a limit per child first</h2>

Decide how much you're willing to spend on each child before you look at anything. Write it down, and include wrapping, batteries and delivery. A limit makes it easier to say no.

<h2 id="2-rule">2. Try a simple gift rule</h2>

Many families use a rule such as "something they want, something they need, something to wear and something to read". It keeps the number of gifts small and each one meaningful.

<h2 id="3-list">3. Make a list and check what they already have</h2>

Before you shop, check the toy cupboard. Ask older kids for a short wish list, and ask grandparents and relatives to choose from it. That avoids doubles.

<h2 id="4-sales">4. Use the sale dates</h2>

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Black Friday | Friday 27 November 2026 |
| Cyber Monday | Monday 30 November 2026 |
| Boxing Day | Saturday 26 December 2026 |

Many retailers run sales in the weeks before Christmas. Note today's price for each gift, and only buy if the sale price is lower than your record. Check delivery cut-off dates, because late orders may not arrive by Christmas. Our [Black Friday baby deals guide](/blog/black-friday-baby-deals-australia-2026/) explains how to avoid fake discounts.

<h2 id="5-second-hand">5. Buy some gifts second-hand</h2>

Books, puzzles, bikes and dress-up clothes are often almost new. Check for recalls, missing pieces and damage, and avoid second-hand items with small or loose parts for toddlers.

<h2 id="6-experiences">6. Give experiences, not just things</h2>

A trip to the zoo, a movie voucher, swimming lessons or a day at the beach costs less than a big toy and makes memories. For toddlers, a picnic and a walk to the park can be a great gift.

<h2 id="7-pool">7. Pool gifts for one bigger item</h2>

If a child wants a bigger gift, such as a ride-on or a cubby house, suggest that relatives chip in together. It's better than lots of small items nobody wants.

<h2 id="8-bnpl">8. Be careful with buy now, pay later</h2>

Spreading a payment can be handy, but late fees and missed payments can cost more than you save. Only use it if you're sure you can pay on time, and count the repayments in your budget.

<h2 id="9-wrap">9. Keep wrapping and extras cheap</h2>

Use recycled paper, fabric or paper bags. Check you have batteries, because many toys don't include them. These small costs add up.

<h2 id="10-after">10. Plan for after Christmas</h2>

Boxing Day and January sales can be a good time to buy birthday gifts and next year's presents, if you can store them. Note the dates in your calendar.

<h2 id="safety">Safe gifts for little kids</h2>

- Follow the age label on every toy.
- Avoid small parts for children under 3.
- Check button batteries are secured with a screw.
- Keep small magnets away from young children.
- Check [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au) for recalls.

<h2 id="faq">Christmas budget FAQ</h2>

### How much should I spend on Christmas gifts for my kids?
It depends on your budget. Set a limit per child before you start, and stick to it.

### When is the best time to buy Christmas gifts?
Start early, track prices, and compare them with Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Boxing Day sales. Don't leave it until the last week.

### Is second-hand OK for kids' gifts?
Yes, for many items, if they're in good condition, have all their parts and haven't been recalled.

### What are cheap Christmas gift ideas for toddlers?
Board books, chunky crayons, play dough, bubbles, bath toys and a picnic or outing.

### Is buy now, pay later a good idea for Christmas?
Only if you can pay it off on time. Late fees and missed payments can make it more expensive.
