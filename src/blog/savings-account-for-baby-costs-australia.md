A baby changes your budget in two ways: there are big one-off costs before bub arrives, and steady new costs after. A separate savings account for those costs makes it easier to see what you've set aside, and harder to dip into it by accident.

This guide explains how to choose a savings account for baby costs in Australia, what to check before you open one, and a simple way to build a baby fund. It's general information, not financial advice, and it doesn't recommend any particular bank or product. *Last updated 24 November 2026.*

**In this guide:** [Why a separate account](#why) · [What to save for](#what-for) · [What to check](#check) · [Bonus rates explained](#bonus) · [A simple plan](#plan) · [Mistakes to avoid](#mistakes) · [FAQ](#faq)

<h2 id="why">Why open a separate account for baby costs?</h2>

- **You can see your progress.** A named account for "baby" is easier to track than a pile of money in your everyday account.
- **It's harder to spend by accident.** Keeping it separate from your card account adds a small barrier.
- **You can automate it.** A regular transfer on payday builds the fund without you having to think about it.
- **It works for two.** Many banks offer joint accounts, so both parents can see and add to it.

<h2 id="what-for">What to save for</h2>

Make a quick list with three groups:

1. **Before the birth.** Gear such as a pram, car seat and cot, plus the hospital bag. Our [newborn and baby essentials checklist](/blog/newborn-essentials-checklist-australia/) and [baby registry checklist](/blog/baby-registry-checklist-australia/) show what you actually need.
2. **Around the birth.** Any out-of-pocket medical costs, time off work and your first weeks at home. Ask your doctor or hospital what you're likely to pay, and check your health cover if you have it.
3. **After the birth.** Nappies, feeding, clothing, childcare and the things that keep coming. Our [first-year baby budget](/blog/first-year-baby-budget-australia/) helps you plan these.

Government payments and support can change what you need to save. Check what you may be eligible for on the [Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/) website.

<h2 id="check">What to check before you open a savings account</h2>

Rates change often, so look at the account's rules and fees, not just the headline number.

- **The rate and how it's made up.** Many accounts have a base rate plus a bonus rate. See the next section.
- **Fees.** Some accounts have monthly fees or charges, so check the product information.
- **Access.** Check how quickly you can move money out, and whether withdrawals affect the bonus.
- **Joint account option**, if you and your partner will save together.
- **Government protection.** Deposits in Australian banks, credit unions and building societies are covered by the Financial Claims Scheme, up to a limit per person, per institution. Check the current limit on the [Financial Claims Scheme website](https://www.fcs.gov.au/).
- **How easy it is to link.** An account that links to your main account makes automatic transfers simple.

You can compare accounts using the Australian Government's [MoneySmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/) guidance on savings accounts, and by reading each bank's product information.

<h2 id="bonus">Bonus interest and introductory rates, explained</h2>

A high advertised rate often comes with conditions. Typical ones include:

- **Depositing a minimum amount each month.**
- **Making no withdrawals** in that month.
- **Growing your balance** each month.
- **Introductory rates** that drop after a set period.

Miss a condition in any month and you may only get the lower base rate. For a baby fund, that matters, because you might need to withdraw money for a pram or a hospital bill. If you expect to dip into the fund, a simpler account with a steady rate can suit you better than a bonus account with strict rules.

<h2 id="plan">A simple plan for a baby fund</h2>

1. **Write down your list** from the three groups above, and add a rough cost to each item.
2. **Decide on a monthly amount** you can afford without stress. Even a small amount helps, and you can increase it later.
3. **Set up an automatic transfer** for the day after payday.
4. **Use sales to spend less.** Plan big purchases around sale events and record prices first. See our guides on [saving money on baby gear](/blog/save-money-on-baby-gear-australia/) and [Black Friday baby deals](/blog/black-friday-baby-deals-australia-2026/).
5. **Review every few months.** Check the rate, the conditions and your progress. If a better account suits you, you can switch.

<h2 id="mistakes">Mistakes to avoid</h2>

- **Choosing on the rate alone.** The conditions matter as much as the number.
- **Leaving money in an introductory rate** after it ends without checking what you now earn.
- **Putting everything in one pot.** Keep emergency savings separate from baby savings.
- **Waiting for the perfect amount.** Starting small is better than not starting.
- **Overbuying early.** A large fund isn't a reason to buy items you don't need yet.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### Do I need a special savings account for a baby?
No. Any savings account works. A separate account just makes the money easier to track and harder to spend by accident.

### When should I start saving for a baby?
As early as you can. If you're already pregnant, start now and focus on the big one-off items first.

### Is a bonus savings account a good choice for baby savings?
It can be, if you can meet the monthly conditions. If you expect to withdraw money for baby gear or bills, a simpler account may suit you better.

### Is my money safe in a savings account?
Deposits in Australian banks, credit unions and building societies are covered by the Financial Claims Scheme up to a limit. Check the current limit and which institutions are covered on the scheme's website.

### How much should I save for a baby?
It depends on your situation. Make a list of what you need and what it costs, then work backwards from your due date to a monthly amount.

### Can I open a savings account in my baby's name?
Many banks offer children's savings accounts, usually opened by a parent or guardian. Check each bank's rules, and compare them using MoneySmart before you choose.
